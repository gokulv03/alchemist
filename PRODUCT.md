# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Curious consumers and the general public — home enthusiasts, casual drinkers, and aspiring home bartenders, not industry professionals. They arrive with a starting point (a spirit they own or like, e.g. gin) and want to understand what to *do* with it. Context is exploratory and often mobile: standing at a home bar, deciding what to make tonight, or browsing out of curiosity. They are not in a hurry to "complete a task" so much as to discover, get inspired, and learn. Low tolerance for jargon or gatekeeping; high reward for delight and a sense of possibility.

## Product Purpose

Alchemist is a discovery and reference product for the world of alcoholic beverages. Starting from any spirit, it reveals the full space of consumption: how to drink it neat or simply, what it pairs and mixes with, and every cocktail built on it. It maps the combinatorial web between spirits, mixers, and recipes so a single ingredient opens into dozens of possibilities. Success looks like a user landing on one spirit and leaving with several new things they're excited to make — and the product feeling memorable and crafted enough that they show it to a friend. The goal is to **impress** as much as to inform.

## Brand Personality

**Bold, magical, alive.** The "alchemist" metaphor is literal: combination, transformation, and a little wonder. Voice is confident and inviting, never snobbish — it democratizes bartending knowledge rather than guarding it. Tone is playful and vivid, leaning into the theatre of mixing (the pour, the transformation, the reveal) without becoming gimmicky. The interface should feel expressive and motion-rich, surprising in small ways, and energetic — closer to a vivid maker's lab than a quiet recipe archive. It should evoke curiosity and delight.

## Anti-references

- **The dark speakeasy / moody-bar cliché.** No near-black backgrounds with gold serif and dim candlelight. This is the first-order reflex for any alcohol product and the user has explicitly ruled out dark-mode-by-default. Avoid it.
- **The generic AI-SaaS look.** No cream/sand/parchment "warm editorial" body backgrounds, no gradient text, no identical icon-heading-text card grids repeated endlessly, no tiny tracked-uppercase eyebrows above every section.
- **The warm-cream recipe-card site** (the second-order reflex once dark is ruled out). Avoid the AllRecipes/Pinterest warm-neutral content-mill aesthetic. "Warmth" must come from accent, type, and imagery — not a beige body.
- Not a sterile data table or spreadsheet of ingredients. Information is a doorway to delight, not a database dump.

## Design Principles

- **Transformation is the hero.** The core magic is one input opening into many outputs (a spirit → ways to drink → mixers → cocktails). Make that branching/combination feel visible and alive, not buried in lists.
- **Invite, don't gatekeep.** Approachable to a total beginner; never condescending to an enthusiast. Plain language over connoisseur jargon; explain the why.
- **Delight earns the showcase.** Every surface should have at least one crafted, memorable moment — motion, materiality, or reveal — because impressing the user is an explicit goal. But delight serves discovery; it never blocks it.
- **Discovery over completion.** Optimize for wandering and serendipity, not task-funnel efficiency. Strong cross-links between spirits, mixers, and cocktails so there's always a tempting next step.
- **Vivid, not loud.** Bold and saturated, but composed — energy with control, never overstimulating.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG AA baseline:** body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, semantic structure.
- **Motion-forward but safe:** motion is central to the brand, so `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is mandatory on every animation, with a crossfade or instant-state alternative. Reveals must enhance already-visible content, never gate it.
- **Light-first, not dark-by-default.** A light or saturated-brand theme is the default; any dark treatment is a deliberate choice, not the baseline.
- Responsive and mobile-first, since much usage happens phone-in-hand at a home bar.
- **Responsible-consumption awareness:** present alcohol content without glamorizing excess; copy should be inclusive of those who drink moderately.
